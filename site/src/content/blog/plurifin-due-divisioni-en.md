PluriFin started as a single app: Portfolio Manager, a tool for reading a portfolio spread
across several brokers without having to piece it together by hand every time. From today
the studio has two divisions — and it is worth explaining why, and why this is not a change
of direction.

## Finance: Portfolio Manager

It stays what it has always been, and it carries on. Eleven brokers read natively, plus a
generic CSV reader for everything else. Your portfolio stays encrypted on your device:
there is no PluriFin server to hand it to, and exporting it to a portable file is always
one tap away. Six languages, like everything else here.

## Games: ROLLQUEST

This is the new part, and it is only fair to say up front where it stands: **it is in
development**. There is no release date, and we are not inventing one. When there is
something to play, you will read it here.

You roll a die, you climb a tower, you grow stronger. Five heroes, four minigames, and one
rule written before anything else: **players who never spend must still be able to reach
the top**. That is not a press-release line — it is a design constraint. If a balancing
choice betrays it, we change the balancing, not the rule.

Every asset is drawn by hand, from scratch: the Tower, the heroes, the dice. Nothing
bought, no shortcuts.

## Why two such different things

They are not alike, and that is the point. But the method is the same, and it is the only
one I know: you work on a thing until it actually works, and then you ship it. Not before.

There is one person behind PluriFin. No investors, no board to please. Which means things
ship when they are ready — and that when something is not ready, like ROLLQUEST today, we
say so.
