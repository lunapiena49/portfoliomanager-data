Keeping a portfolio split across several brokers in a spreadsheet means rewriting by hand what each statement says in its own way. Anyone who tires of that looks for an app, and before loading statements into one it is worth asking it five questions. Under each is the answer from Portfolio Manager, the app from PluriFin Finance: readers can check it against the web demo and the privacy policy, and demand the same clarity from the others.

## 1. Where the data ends up

There are three possible answers:

- **On the app maker’s servers**: convenient for anyone with several devices, but the portfolio lives in someone else’s house
- **On the device, unencrypted**: that is the spreadsheet’s case, readable by any program with access to the files
- **On the device, encrypted**: the key is created on the phone and stays there

Portfolio Manager is in the third group. Portfolios, positions and goals stay on the phone, encrypted with AES-256, with the key guarded by the operating system. PluriFin has no archive to keep them in and does not ask anyone to open an account. The web demo is the exception, and says so: its data stays in the browser, unencrypted.

A free app that stores portfolios on its own servers has a cost to cover. The next question is what covers it.

## 2. How the statements get in

There are two routes: hand the app the broker’s credentials, or import the files the broker exports. Portfolio Manager knows only the second: it does not connect to accounts and does not execute orders.

The app reads the CSV files of eleven brokers: Interactive Brokers, Charles Schwab, Fidelity, TD Ameritrade (now part of Charles Schwab), E\*TRADE, Robinhood, Vanguard, DEGIRO, Trading 212, XTB and Revolut. For the others there is a generic reader, which needs only the ticker symbol and one column among quantity, value and price. It also accepts PDFs, and tries to extract the table from them.

Any app should be asked whether importing one’s own statement can be tried before paying. In Portfolio Manager it can: the seven-day trial has every feature and asks for no credit card. In the web demo it cannot: there, positions are entered by hand, five at most.

## 3. Who reads the questions put to artificial intelligence

A portfolio analysis written by a model requires sending the portfolio to whoever runs the model. It is worth knowing which servers it passes through and who stores it.

In Portfolio Manager the analysis is optional and uses Google Gemini, with a key the user enters personally. The question and the portfolio’s composition (securities, quantities, average prices) go from the phone to Google without passing through PluriFin, which does not store them. The same holds for real-time prices: the ticker symbols go to the provider the user chooses.

## 4. What the app does not do

Portfolio Manager does not execute orders and is not a broker. The artificial intelligence analyses are pointers generated by a model: they are not personalized financial advice and may contain errors.

Nor does the app calculate taxes. The gains and losses it shows are arithmetic: current price minus average price, times quantity. They take no account of capital gains tax, stamp duty on securities or the offsetting of capital losses. For tax returns, the app’s financial disclaimer points to an accountant or an authorized tax assistance center (CAF).

To take the data elsewhere there is “Export my data”, which produces a JSON file with all the content.

## 5. What it costs, and what happens when the payments stop

The industry’s vice is the trial that asks for a card and the subscription that starts by itself when no one is looking. In Portfolio Manager the trial lasts seven days and never sees its user’s card: when it ends, no charge starts by itself.

After that there are four plans, the same for everyone, from 4.99 euros a year to 49.99 euros once for the family. No plan contains advertising. Renewals, cancellation and refunds are on the [Pricing](/en/pricing) page.

When the trial ends, a free version remains. Positions are entered by hand, and rebalancing and market data keep working; broker import, goals and artificial intelligence stop until a plan is chosen. Either way the data stays on the phone, exportable.

## Where to start

The [web demo](/app/) opens in the browser with nothing to install, within stated limits: five positions entered by hand, no import, no goals and no artificial intelligence. Anyone staying with the spreadsheet can take the five questions along: they apply to any app.
